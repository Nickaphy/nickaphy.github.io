Vim Motions Everywhere

2026-09-24
  • tooling

Vim motions have been a huge part of how I work. I have them toggled on or installed in every environment that supports it. But there's a specific bad feeling that comes from landing in an app that doesn't — Discord, a random GTK dialog, a browser text field — and suddenly your fingers don't know what to do. That feeling sent me down a rabbit hole, and I came out the other side with what I'd call the actual solution, not another half-measure.

It's Not About hjkl

My first instinct was to just use arrow keys instead of fighting broken vim emulation everywhere. That's when I noticed the real bottleneck: it was never the keys, it was the distance. Reaching off the home row to hit an arrow key costs real time, every single press. That's the actual reason vim's hjkl caught on in the first place — not the modal editing, just that your hand never leaves home row.

So the goal became: get arrow-key navigation onto the home row, without depending on any single app's vim plugin being any good.

Kanata

Kanata is a free, open-source key remapper written in Rust. It sits between your keyboard and your operating system, intercepting every keypress before your OS ever sees it:

Key press -> kanata intercepts -> kanata sends whatever you configured -> OS

The part that makes this actually work everywhere: kanata operates at the kernel's evdev/uinput level — below X11, Wayland, and your window manager entirely. It doesn't know or care whether you're in Discord, a terminal, or a browser field, because it's not talking to any of those. It's talking to the kernel, the same way your physical keyboard does. That's the difference between this and every app-level "vim mode" I'd tried before: those only work if that specific app chose to implement them well. This works regardless.

The Setup: Caps Lock as a Mode Switch

With a modifier key — I used Caps Lock, since nobody needs its original job — held down, kanata activates a second layer of key bindings. Release it, and you're typing normally. Hold it, and you're effectively in vim's "normal mode":

Key (while holding Caps) Action
h j k l Left / Down / Up / Right
w Jump forward one word
b Jump backward one word
u Undo
y Copy (acts on the current selection)
d, d Delete the current line
d, i, w Delete the word under the cursor
v Toggle visual mode — hjkl/w/b then extend a selection instead of just moving; d/y act on it and drop back out

None of this reimplements vim's actual text-object engine. d, i, w isn't a real "delete inner word" — it's "select the word under the cursor using the same Ctrl+Left / Ctrl+Shift+Right shortcut every OS text field already supports, then delete." It rides entirely on shortcuts apps already implement, rather than trying to out-build vim's parser from scratch. That constraint is exactly why it works in Discord and a browser field and not just in a real editor.
